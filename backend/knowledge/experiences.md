# SAD Marketing — Fixed-Term Contract

Mohamed worked at SAD Marketing as a Web Developer under a fixed-term contract from December 2025 to January 2026, following his final-year engineering internship at the same company. SAD Marketing specializes in marketing and market research, including market studies, catchment area studies, customer prospecting, and CAP studies. Mohamed was primarily involved in the technical side of the company rather than directly participating in its marketing activities.

During this contract, Mohamed worked mainly autonomously and collaborated directly with the Lead Developer. His main objective was to automate the verification, processing, and import of customer data. The company received Excel files from different clients, and the structure and content of these files could vary significantly between clients. Previously, the data verification process was performed manually before the information was imported into the company's database.

Mohamed developed several Python scripts that worked together as an automated data-processing pipeline. The process began by retrieving data from the received files and validating the information. The validation included checking data types, detecting duplicate records, identifying empty cells, applying client-specific rules, and verifying other consistency requirements. The processed data was then subjected to calculations and comparisons, including comparisons with the previous month and the same month of the previous year, in order to identify potentially inconsistent values.

After the processing stage, the system generated a verification report and sent the results by email to the responsible person. The responsible person could review and validate the results before the data was integrated into the company's database. This workflow reduced the need for manual verification while keeping a validation step before importing the data.

The main technical difficulty was the heterogeneity of the customer data. Different clients could provide files with different structures, columns, formatting, validation rules, and processing requirements. Mohamed therefore had to introduce client-specific processing while maintaining a sufficiently automated overall workflow. This experience exposed him to practical data-processing and automation problems where incoming data cannot be assumed to follow one consistent schema.

# SAD Marketing — Final-Year Engineering Internship

Mohamed completed his final-year engineering internship at SAD Marketing from May 2025 to November 2025. The internship lasted six months and his position was Web Developer. The main technologies used during the project were Vue.js, Flask, PostgreSQL, Python, and Git.

The project consisted of designing and developing an employee time-management application. The objective was to allow employees to record the amount of time they spent working on different projects. The collected information gave the company a global view of workload distribution across different areas and helped provide more accurate estimates for future projects.

The team consisted of one Lead Developer, two developers, one Data Analyst, and Mohamed. Mohamed was the only developer working directly on this particular application because the other developers were working on different solutions for the company. As a result, he worked with a relatively high degree of autonomy while regularly discussing technical decisions with the other developers and the Lead Developer when he needed feedback or validation.

Mohamed worked on the application from its design through to deployment. His responsibilities included designing and developing the application, building the frontend with Vue.js, developing the backend with Flask, working with PostgreSQL, implementing application functionality, migrating existing data, testing and fixing issues, and deploying the application on the company's internal server. After deployment, he also collected feedback from users and corrected problems discovered during real-world usage.

One of the main technical difficulties was migrating historical data from the company's previous platform. The old system and the new application used different database structures and relationships. Mohamed therefore had to understand the existing data structure, understand the new database structure, determine how the old data corresponded to the new structure, transform the data where necessary, and migrate the historical information without losing important data. This gave him practical experience working with existing data and systems rather than starting with a completely new dataset.

The development process was similar to Scrum, although it did not follow formal Scrum ceremonies exactly. Daily meetings were used to discuss completed work, upcoming tasks, and potential blockers. The team also held a monthly meeting to review the previous month and define the main tasks for the following month. Mohamed worked autonomously on most of his tasks but regularly communicated with the developers and Lead Developer when he needed an opinion on a technical decision or wanted to validate an implementation choice. Git was used for version control and code sharing.

At the end of the internship, Mohamed deployed the application on the company's internal server. The project therefore exposed him to the complete development lifecycle, from design and development through testing and deployment to collecting user feedback and fixing production issues. It also strengthened his practical experience with Flask, Vue.js, PostgreSQL, full-stack development, data migration, deployment, and working with users after an application has been released.

# Walden Digital — Frontend Developer Internship

Mohamed completed a frontend development internship at Walden Digital from June 2024 to September 2024. The company specializes in digital solutions and web application development. During the internship, Mohamed worked as a Frontend Developer with Angular, TypeScript, Flask, HTTP APIs, Figma, and Mercurial.

The main project was a frontend redesign. Before the redesign, the company used WEBDEV for the frontend and Flask for the backend and APIs. The existing frontend was considered difficult to maintain and evolve, and the previous technology also imposed limitations on visual and UI development. The goal was therefore to replace the existing frontend with a more modern and maintainable Angular application while keeping the Flask backend and APIs.

The team consisted of one Project Manager, two developers, and Mohamed. He worked relatively autonomously on his assigned tasks and discussed conceptual or technical decisions with the developers when necessary. The internship was conducted in person, which allowed direct communication and coordination with the team.

Mohamed's main responsibility was developing the new Angular frontend based on Figma designs. His work included implementing graphical components, creating reusable UI components, following the provided designs, organizing the frontend using a feature-driven architecture, and connecting the Angular frontend to the existing Flask backend through HTTP requests.

A significant part of the work involved creating reusable graphical components that could be used in different parts of the application. Instead of duplicating frontend logic, Mohamed organized components so that they could be reused across multiple features. The frontend was also structured using a feature-driven architecture, organizing code around application functionality rather than grouping everything only by technical type. This approach made the code easier to navigate and helped organize the application as it grew.

The Angular frontend communicated with the Flask backend through HTTP APIs. Angular sent requests to the existing backend and consumed the returned data within the application. This gave Mohamed additional experience integrating a modern frontend with an existing backend rather than developing both sides of the application from scratch.

One of the initial difficulties was that Mohamed had never worked with WEBDEV before the internship. Before beginning the redesign, he had to understand how the existing frontend worked, how the application was structured, what limitations the existing technology had, what functionality needed to be preserved, and what could be improved in the new frontend. This experience taught him how to understand an existing system before replacing part of it and how to approach frontend modernization in an existing application.

The internship strengthened Mohamed's Angular and TypeScript skills and gave him experience with frontend redesign, reusable components, Figma-based development, feature-driven architecture, HTTP API integration, legacy-system understanding, and autonomous work within a development team. Mercurial was used for version control and code sharing.

# RABBAH SOFT — Full-Stack Developer Internship

Mohamed completed his professional bachelor's degree final internship at RABBAH SOFT from April 2022 to June 2022 in Casablanca, Morocco. RABBAH SOFT is a small IT company specializing in IT solutions, including web and mobile applications, solution integration, and IT consulting. His position was Full-Stack Developer, and the main technologies used were Java, Spring Boot, Angular, PostgreSQL, Hibernate, Spring Data JPA, REST APIs, and GitLab.

Mohamed developed a ticket management system using Spring Boot for the backend, Angular for the frontend, and PostgreSQL for data storage. The application was designed for both clients and employees. Clients could create tickets describing their requests or problems, while employees could view incoming tickets, take ownership of them, process requests, resolve problems, and close tickets after resolution. The objective was to provide the company with a ticketing solution adapted specifically to its workflow and its clients rather than relying on a generic tool such as Jira.

The team consisted of one Lead Developer, one developer, and two interns. Mohamed worked primarily with the other intern on feature development. When architectural or design questions arose, they consulted the developer or Lead Developer for guidance and validation. GitLab was used for version control and code sharing.

Mohamed worked mainly on the backend, including business logic, database design, ticket creation and modification, ticket assignment, user access rights, and PostgreSQL integration. He also worked on the Angular frontend. Because there was no existing UI mockup at the beginning of the project, he also participated in decisions concerning the ergonomics and organization of the interface.

The backend followed a layered architecture based on Controller, Service, and Repository layers. Controllers handled API requests, Services contained business logic, and Repositories handled access to the database. The application used Hibernate and Spring Data JPA to simplify communication between the Spring Boot application and PostgreSQL. Mohamed created backend entities and repositories that allowed the application to interact with the database without manually writing every SQL query.

Some features took longer than initially expected because there was no predefined UI design at the beginning of the project. The team therefore needed to spend additional time considering the application's ergonomics and interface organization. To reduce the impact on the schedule, the team prioritized the most important features first. When Mohamed was uncertain about an architectural or design decision, he discussed it with the developer or Lead Developer before continuing, which helped avoid spending significant time implementing an approach that would later need to be changed.

The main technical difficulty was adapting to a new professional environment while learning several technologies simultaneously. It was Mohamed's first full-stack development internship and his first professional experience with Spring Boot, Angular, and GitLab. He already had a foundation in Java and JavaScript, which helped him learn the new technologies. By the end of the internship, he had contributed to the project through both backend and frontend development and gained experience working within a professional software-development team.

This internship gave Mohamed his first significant professional experience with a full-stack application. He learned how to build a backend with Spring Boot, structure a backend using Controller/Service/Repository layers, work with PostgreSQL, use Hibernate and Spring Data JPA, develop frontend features with Angular, design an interface without an existing mockup, use GitLab, prioritize features under time constraints, and adapt to an unfamiliar technology stack.
